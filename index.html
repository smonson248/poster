Motivation

Two clinical challenges
Therapeutics
Only ~1% of engineered therapeutic nanoparticles (NP) reach their target in solid tumors, in part due to immune recognition and clearance.
Diagnostics
Tumors exploit extracellular vesicles (EVs) to modulate the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), making it difficult to predict patient prognoses.

HypothesisNanoparticle trafficking in the TIME is governed by coupling between cell-extrinsic immune-recognition processes and cell-intrinsic biophysical mechanisms of cellular uptake, shaping tissue-level behavior.

ImpactImproved therapeutic design principles and non-invasive diagnostics.

ObjectiveDetermine how extrinsic factors influence immune recognition and uptake of nanoparticles.

Conclusions and Impact

Conclusions
The coupling of cell-extrinsic (complement activity and nanoparticle surface chemistry) and cell-intrinsic (membrane and receptor properties) factors drives immune receptor engagement and membrane remodeling.
▼
Scientific impact
Our work delineates key physical mechanisms governing NP recognition and uptake under heterogeneous immune and biophysical conditions.
▼
Clinical impact

These findings motivate integrating immune, membrane, and single-EV profiling biomarkers to inform patient-specific diagnostics and therapy optimization to tackle inter- and intra-patient heterogeneity.

These physics may govern heterogeneous signaling responsible for (1) malignant and metastatic transformation via exosome trafficking and (2) reduced therapeutic efficacy in tumors.
